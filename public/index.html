Spins, pulses & equations Model notes ↗

01 / Two nuclear spins · §7.7.1–2

A is the first bit (control, ¹H); B is the second bit (target, ¹³C). |0⟩ and |1⟩ are Z eigenstates with eigenvalues +1 and −1. We use a rotating frame for each spin and the weak-coupling, liquid-state approximation. J = 215 Hz is the proton–carbon coupling discussed in §7.7.4.

Hfree/ℏ = πδᴀZᴀ + πδʙZʙ + (πJ/2) ZᴀZʙ
U(t) = exp(−iHt/ℏ)

J and δ are ordinary frequencies in Hz, not angular frequencies. δʙ = 0 here. During ideal RF pulses, free evolution is neglected. Dipolar coupling is averaged away.

02 / Rotations & CNOT · §7.7.2–3

HRF/ℏ = [ΔZ + Ω(cos φ X + sin φ Y)] / 2
Rn(θ) = exp(−iθ n·σ/2)
UZZ(t) = exp(−iπJt ZᴀZʙ/2)
tCNOT = 1/(2J)

Hʙ → UZZ → Rᴀz(−π/2)Rʙz(−π/2) → Hʙ gives CNOT at tCNOT, up to global phase. A Hadamard can be compiled as H ∝ Ry(π/2)Rz(π). Z rotations are ideal phase-frame changes. The local corrections are fixed as t varies.

03 / Refocusing & state preparation · §7.7.3

Uecho = Rᴀx(π) U(t/2) Rᴀx(π) U(t/2)
ρ = (1 − ε) I/4 + ε|ψ⟩⟨ψ|

Conjugating Zᴀ and ZᴀZʙ with an X pulse reverses their signs. Two π pulses cancel both terms here, up to global phase; a nonzero Zʙ term would survive. Turn the pulses off to compare ordinary precession.

Pseudopure states are supplied as prepared inputs. Temporal/spatial labeling is not simulated. ε = 10⁻⁵ illustrates signal suppression; it is not calculated from a particular field or temperature. The full state is mixed even when its deviation resembles a pure-state calculation. For |+0⟩ at the ideal CNOT output, this family is entangled only if ε > 1/3.

04 / Ensemble readout · §7.7.2, §7.7.4

S(t) = Tr[ρ(t)(Xᴀ + iYᴀ)]
S(t) = e−t/T₂ ∑ ak ei2πfₖt
fₖ = δᴀ ± J/2 · FWHM = 1/(πT₂)

The receiver uses S = ⟨X⟩ + i⟨Y⟩, a quadrature convention differing from Eq. (7.143) by phase and conjugation. Each line is weighted by a single-spin coherence; a line can be absent. The spectrum is the analytic infinite-time transform with exp(−i2πft), divided by εT₂, not an FFT of the displayed window.

Independent pure dephasing acts on both spins during readout; T₁ is infinite in this model. Matrix entries are computed directly, not reconstructed from a tomography experiment. The overlap F = ⟨ψideal|ρ|ψideal⟩ compares with a pure ideal output for this input, not a process fidelity.

Model scope: This is a two-spin teaching model of Nielsen & Chuang §7.7, not a full NMR spectrometer. Ideal selective pulses and a weak ZZ coupling are assumed; gate modes exclude relaxation and RF imperfections. Liquid-state NMR uses highly mixed ensembles with small detectable deviations. A pseudopure signal does not by itself demonstrate entanglement or scalable quantum computation. Full labeling protocols, tomography, and the quantum-search experiment of §7.7.4 are not implemented.
